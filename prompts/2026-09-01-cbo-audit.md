### 2. Suitability for govblock

**A bill page's Cost estimate section — yes, in two layers, and the second
layer is the problem.**

- *Layer 1 (free, complete, today):* for 1,046 bills of the 119th the
  BILLSTATUS zip already gives `title · pubDate · description (stage) ·
  url` per estimate. That renders a truthful section — "CBO estimated this
  bill as ordered reported by House Agriculture on 2026-02-26 → link" —
  with zero requests to CBO, and it is refreshed by the same nightly
  `billstatus.mjs` pass lane C runs. It also tells the page which stage was
  estimated, which matters more than the number for a bill that changed in
  committee.
- *Layer 2 (the numbers):* the summary table — direct spending, revenues,
  deficit effect for the first year, 5 years, 10 years; the five yes/no
  flags (PAYGO, mandates, long-term deficits) — is in the page HTML and the
  PDF, and is exactly what a card wants. **It is unreachable by a
  harvester**: cbo.gov's HTML and `system/files` PDFs are behind DataDome
  (three blocks, measured; the Internet Archive is blocked since ~Aug 2026
  too). The honest options are (a) a human pastes the summary table for
  the few bills that matter (HR 1 and the appropriations bills, a few dozen
  a year), (b) ask CBO — `communications@cbo.gov` welcomes feedback on the
  open-data repository, and cost-estimate summary tables in that repository
  is a reasonable request from a downstream site, or (c) the Internet
  Archive for estimates published before Aug 2026 (coverage in 1.2a). I
  recommend (a) for the handful and (b) as a letter; not (c) as a pipeline.
- Member pages: **nothing** — CBO publishes by bill and committee, never by
  member.

**A Budget surface — yes, from the repository alone, and it is the better
product.** `ten_year_budget` (336 variables × FY2025–FY2036, 2026-02
vintage: revenues by source, outlays by category, deficits, debt, and the
*changes since the prior baseline* by cause), `historical_budget` (66
variables, FY1962–FY2025: the same series actual), `long_term_budget`
(FY2025–FY2056 as shares of GDP), `trust_fund` (OASI, DI, HI, SMI, military
and civilian retirement balances), `revenue_detail`, `tax_parameters`
(brackets, rates, thresholds by calendar year — a "what the code says"
table), and `spending_detail` (**1,712 Treasury accounts × 11 years**, with
agency, bureau, function, discretionary/mandatory). All long-format, all
schema'd, all public domain. Vintages make a "what changed since last
February" view free. The `tin` account key joins to appropriations bills by
account title (the bills name accounts, not TINs) and to agencies by the
`agency`/`bureau` strings — a join lane P can do by name for the 12
appropriations bills, not something the data does for us.

**The newsroom's Congress desk — yes, from the feed.** ~3 new estimates a
working day, each with bill number, stage, and link; poll hourly, filter to
bill-titled items, join on the bill number to our `Bills` row (the feed has
no bill id; BILLSTATUS has both). The 6-of-30 report items are a second
desk ("CBO published: Effects of Automatic Stabilizers…") if wanted.

**Licence and attribution.** Repository: public domain (17 U.S.C. § 105),
no attribution required, CBO asks only that modifications be identified.
BILLSTATUS: US Government work, public domain. The feed's text is CBO's own
publication metadata; link back to the publication, which we do anyway.

**Harvest cost, at Lane A's Parquet ratios.** Lane A measured Neon 75.6 GB
→ lake 1.82 GB for 156.7 M rows (~41× on Postgres bytes, indexes and TOAST
included) and zstd-3 at ~4.9× on legislative text. These CSVs are numeric
long-format with repeated `variable` strings, which dictionary-encode well;
call it 5–8× on the 41 MB, so **5–8 MB of Parquet for the whole
repository**, one file per dataset per vintage, refreshed when CBO commits
(2 commits in its life; poll the GitHub API daily, free).
Cost-estimate metadata: 0 requests — it is inside the BILLSTATUS pass we
already run; 1,115 rows. Feed: 1 request/hour, 30 rows each, ~80 new/day
across both desks after dedupe; trivial. Summary-table numbers: not
harvestable — see above.
